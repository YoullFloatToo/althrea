Cael stepped through the tavern doors and into the night’s chaos within. Of course, he expected to see plenty of folks in attendance tonight. Yet, he wasn’t prepared for the total overtake of the Inn’s tavern.
 
The room was packed so full of people, he could scarcely make his way past the entrance. If not for the doors opening outward, he wouldn’t have been able to get in. He pushed his way through the mob of people, bumping shoulders and nearly tripping over fallen chairs hidden in the sea of bodies. The room was filled with a smokey haze that floated above the crowd, lightly stinging his eyes. He could see at the far end of the room a fire roaring in its place below the chimney, little plumes of smoke streamed and swirled up through the air from the pipes jutting out of the crowd's mouths. Their voices melted together, creating a crackling buzz of noise almost too sharp to bear. Still, Cael could make out individual voices above the rest. At the other end of the room, a man’s laughter cut through the noise. Just to his side, a woman sighed irritably, clearly engaged in a hushed conversation of gossip with the other women gathered around her. And over it all, a stern woman’s voice shouted from behind the bar, barking orders to the tavern servants.
 
The wooden planks of the tavern's flooring whined and creaked as the twisting horde shifted about on top of them. Chairs and tables scraped across it as the furniture was dragged back and forth, tankards of mead slammed the bar tops as the men cheered and laughed, splashing the floor with bubbling foam. It was a scene Cael was too familiar with, given the near decade long gauntlet of weddings that plagued his childhood as each of his older brothers came of age. Such experience hadn’t swayed Cael’s opinion, however, as he still disliked large gatherings very much so. Yet, this one he had attended willingly, for his business in being there was an opportunity he'd never come across before, and most likely never would again.
 
Cael continued to push through the mass, eventually settling in one of the more spacious gaps, if you could call it that, in the crowd along the back wall. As he scanned the room, glossing over the obnoxious group of men slamming their pints together at the bar, his eyes settled on another man sat next to them, his back facing Cael. He sat almost completely motionless. His long dark hair drawn back, his thick beard visible even from behind. But what was most notable was, of course, the oversized sword strapped to his back. Cael had heard of such swords before. Oversized and too heavy to wield single handedly, but not to the point that they could not be wield at all, although this particular sword was pushing that limit. They were built for a certain type of man, one who intended to keep combat swift and effective, lacking any of the flashy swordplay common amongst the young knights of recent times looking for fame and glory. Even their design reflected that philosophy, being very large but otherwise plain and unornamented.

As Cael watched the man curiously, suddenly a voice erupted from right beside him.
 
“Ha HAAAAA! By the great founding tits ya blundering fool!”  
   
“That’s really what she said! Ha ha! And the other one too. She laid into my arse so good my moon was still red in the morning.”  
   
Two of the most ridiculously put together men Cael had ever seen sat down at the table, which somehow had not yet been taken, just next to where he was standing. Cael stared at them fixedly, hardly believing his eyes. They were more than a sight to see, and the longer Cael stared the more appalled he grew. The bigger of the two, was red in the face and so large that heaps of meat and flesh hung off the sides of his chair. His rather dull and plain-looking armor plating must have cost a small fortune comparable to what a noble knight would pay for his gleaming suit, simply for its sheer size. The smaller of the two men, Cael might have thought to be a child had there not been a glistening waxed mustachio curled across his upper lip. His face barely cleared the top of the table, and Cael could see his short stubby legs hanging off his chair, swinging and kicking wildly below the table.  
   
“You always had a way with women, haven’t ya?” the rounder man bellowed.  
   
“Ye I have a way with them. They took one good look at me comin, and shout AWAY! AWAY!”  
   
“AAHHH HAA HAAAAAAA!”  
   
They pounded the table with their fists which rattled and skipped across the floor, adding more noise to the symphony of voices in the room. It was a wonder that the table didn’t split in half at their pummeling. The two were a pair of buffoons unlike Cael had ever seen. The big one’s laughing came in thunderous wheezes of unbearable volume. His face, for some reason, scrunched up in an expression of absolute despair. The little one fired out rapid chuckles which bubbled out of him in signal of devious intent. He reminded Cael slightly of a rabbit he once saw as a boy, nibbling away at his family's garden. They continued their conversation on the ridiculous antics and rouses they’ve pulled on women for few moments more before noticing Cael staring at them.
 
The large one locked eyes with Cael, and then suddenly exploded once more.
 
"A friend! A frieeeeendd indeed! Come join us now ya tall handsome red haired square jawed tempt-a-man model of a young lad! You must have had a day in getting way out here. As have we." He gestured to the tiny man beside him.
 
"Yes! As have we! Hehehehehehe," the small one repeated, chuckling uncontrollably.
 
Cael continued to stare blankly at the both of them. It was clear they were beyond drunk, and most likely the type of fools Cael avoided at all costs. Loud and crude, obscene, and simply too much in every regard. Yet, there was something about them that Cael could not ignore. There was an energy in their presence that seemed to have a way of disarming a man of his social vigilance. Cael certainly hadn't come here to make friends. Nevertheless, he found himself pulling out the only remaining chair at the table and having a seat.
 
"THAAAATTSSS my booyyyy!" the big one erupted with unnecessary volume.
 
Cael looked around. Now sitting down with his perspective lowered below eye level, the table seemed to be nothing but a forest clearing encircled by a tall barrier of trees. He eyed both of the two men sitting opposite him before offering out his hand.
 
"It is my pleasure to make your acquaintance. I'm Cael."
 
The smaller man grabbed Cael's hand and shook vigorously. "The pleasure is, of course, ours. I'm Bo."
 
Then the large one reached for Cael's hand and shook it as well. "And I'm also Bo. They call us Big Bo and Small Bo."
 
Then Big Bo joined in. "Except he's Small Bo."
 
"And he's Big Bo."
 
"AAAAAAAAAHHHHHHHHHHH!"
 
They once again broke out into an insane fit of laughter, as if their names, which described a size opposite of what they were, was the funniest thing to ever have existed. Cael honestly questioned if sitting down at the table had been the right idea.
 
Small Bo, the larger of the two Bos, suddenly cut in with a serious tone to his voice. "We promise ye we aint drunk. Havent even had one poured out yet. Just got in right before we looked over and saw ya starin at us."
 
Cael stared at the two. "Right," he said flatly, wondering why he even decided to sit down.
 
"Listen lad," Small Bo said, leaning in. "I know why you came here. And it aint for the same reason the rest of this pack of unruly goons showed up," he said gesturing to the immense crowd of people in the tavern.
 
Cael raised an eyebrow. "Think I don't care for coin?"
 
"You'll be armed for that aye?" Small Bo leaned over to the side of the table to check underneath. "Don’t see no long shiny metal hangin off yer hip."
 
Cael felt a slight wave of discomfort. He didn’t like being read into like that.
 
"Nah I know what." Small Bo continued. "Ye came to see if it's true." Then he gestured upwards towards the ceiling at the far end of the room, as if to point to something outside the tavern. "Came to see if there really is one of them black beasts up in those hills." He paused, then continued. "A draeth," he said eerily while shaking his hands in the air.
 
Big Bo suddenly elbowed Small Bo, which didn't do much except to nearly knock himself off his own chair, their masses being so different. "Well don't say it!" he shouted as the chair tipped back upright.
 
"Oh will you drop that already now. You act like something's going to happen," Small Bo said with a note of annoyance at his smaller companion. He looked back at Cael. "I've got it right?"
 
Cael's eyes darted between the two Bos for a moment before he spoke. "And what of the pair of you?" Then he leaned over the side of the table to peer underneath, just as Small Bo had. "Aside from the armor, seems to me there's nothing long and shiny on your end of the table either."
 
"Maybe not shiny but there sure is something long HAHAAAA!" Small Bo said with a laugh. "Why of course! The two of us aint come here for that neither. Can you imagine?" he said, his laugh rising with intensity. "Bo!" he called out to his smaller brother. "Think of it, us two, havin a go at that beast!"
 
Big Bo, now laughing as well, joined in on the idea. "We'd just need to get you on top of it somehow. You'd pin it to the ground so the rest of us could poke him dead!" He said, exploding into a rapid chuckling fit.
 
Small Bo returned the jab. "Yeah? And how about we just feed you to him? One whiffaya and he'd pass out! Then we'd have a go at im'!"
 
"HAA HAAAAA!"
 
The two broke down into another raging fit of laughter that rocked the table. Cael, despite being amused by the conversation, found his thoughts drifting away from the two Bos. His thoughts were somewhere darker than the bright energetic tavern of Greywharf he’d somehow made his way to. That darker place was in the stories. The tales of supposed draeth sightings and encounters that he’d collected. Contemplating them was a difficult task. Sorting out the truths from the falsehoods, and where there were falsehoods, finding the motives behind fabricating them, and through it all, the overbearing weight of reality which settled in his stomach. The worst of it had been the tale that lead him to the very conversation he now took part in. Suddenly Cael wondered if Big Bo and Small Bo really knew exactly what it is they were talking about, and so he questioned.
 
“And what of the breath?” Cael cut in.
 
The two Bos looked at Cael, the imaginary scene dissipating immediately upon the question, their posture dropping low in shame. And it was then that Cael could tell, if not in totality, than in part, that they did believe it, that there really was a black beast lurking up in those hills, which was a thought that made Cael even more curious. _Then why had they come? Not to collect the bounty, not to see if it's true, but what?_ But before he could ask, Bo began to speak.
 
This time, Small Bo was quiet, and it was Big Bo who spoke. “We’d not last against it, lad. We know of what we speak.”
 
They all sat in silence for a few moments, the tavern still raging around them. And then Small Bo spoke again.
 
"So, then you believe it too?"
 
Cael sat back in his seat. His head turned to scan the room briefly, noticing again just how many people were present. He turned back to Small Bo. "I don’t know what to believe anymore. I've studied the subject coming on two years now. I've collected the stories, supposed sightings. Usually, it's clearly nothing more than that, just stories. But sometimes, there's a little bit more to it, makes it hard to write off so easily."
 
"This one of those times?"
 
Cael eyed Small Bo. "This time… It's different."
 
"Different how?"
 
Cael opened his mouth to speak, then closed it, finding it difficult to explain how and why this time was different. To him, it was more of a feeling than anything else. It wasn’t any specific details which he could point out to a second observer. He supposed that came from experience, tracking down the accounts, judging the evidence. Thus, he struggled to convey something as nebulous as a feeling. So instead, he asked a question.
 
“You read the notice posting?”
 
“Right, I did ye. Well, he did.” Small Bo said while gesturing to Big Bo.
 
Cael turned to Big Bo. “And you read the whole thing I presume? Including the small bit at the end.”
 
“The small bit at the end?”
 
Cael dug into the pocket in his trousers and drew out a small slip of folded parchment. Unfolding it, he flattened it out on the table and turned it around to face the Bos.
 
“It’s my own writing, but I copied the notice. This is what it said, word for word.” Then Cael put his finger on the bottom of the sheet, pointing out the text written there. He read aloud. “If it be that you, or any you know, claim to possess a working proficiency in the subject of draethlore, let it be declared that your expertise is not merely welcomed, but requested, with all due gratuity."
 
Small Bo then dug into his own pocket, also drawing forth a slip of paper, which he unfolded and placed on the table. This paper, however, presented the same bounty as Cael’s, but written with far greater penmanship. It was clearly an official copy, pulled straight from which ever notice board it was posted.
 
“This is the official bounty. We know we weren’t supposed to, but we pulled this off the board to take with us.”
 
Then Small Bo put his finger at the bottom of his page. “Lad, I’m not sure where you copied yours from, but there’s no special request at the bottom of ours.”
 
Cael’s head turned in confusion. He reached out to grab Small Bo’s copy and held it up close to his eyes. He was right. Small Bo’s copy didn’t mention anything of the sort. Cael picked up the first slip of paper, the one with his own writing, and held it next to the official copy, comparing the two. Everything was identical. Everything except the part of at the bottom. It was as if the additional request was simply omitted from the official copy. Or had he only imagined it was there on the copy he read? Cael handed Small Bo’s paper back, wondering exactly what was going on. He was sure he copied the writing exactly as it was written. He then reread the special request on the paper he’d brought. When he read it aloud, he stopped short, leaving the last sentence unsaid. Not for any reason in particular, but because he’d covered the general meaning behind the message. This time he reread the message in full. Huh, he thought. He reread the final sentence.
 
"And let it also be known that those who carry lies will not be met with the same warmth of welcome for these are superstitious parts, and our folk take poorly to tales without truth.”
 
He repeated the words in his mind. It was requesting the presence of someone who claimed to know more about draeths. Yet at the same time it was subverting the presence of those who would dare come with tales of falsehood. But wasn’t every tale about a draeth a falsehood in the eyes of the common man? Cael thought about it more, trying to make sense of the stranger thing. Could it be that only some of the posted notices contained this additional message, while the others did not? He wondered if it was meant to attract a certain type of person, but not too many of them. Or perhaps it was meant to sift out any potential frauds. Cael hadn’t come to make friends, but eventually, he would have to ask some of the others if he wanted to confirm his suspicion.
 
Both Cael and Small Bo folded up their papers and slipped them back into their pockets. They all sat there in silence for a few moments, and just as Cael opened his mouth to ask another question. Small Bo said something first.
 
"They say those beasts come from beyond the shade," he said, more to himself than to anyone else. His eyes were empty, fixed to the table top between them all, not looking at it in particular, but rather because there was hardly anywhere else to look without making contact with someone's eyes, letting them know what he was feeling. And then Big Bo joined in. "And that they climb over the wall and fly all the way across Faarlen, to get to places like this."
 
Cael looked back at the two Bos, now much more interested in the two men than he'd been since he first sat down at the table. Cael knew of the myths. Of course, the very existence of draeths is a myth in itself. But there exists a second layer to it, a secret, not so frequented, lower floor, a myth within a myth. For even among the believers, there were those who believed in even more still, and those who did not. The notion that the draeths had somehow come from beyond the shade, which was, as far as anyone knew, where the world ended, was about as obscure a piece of folklore that a man would ever hear, carried far across the continents through whispered voices, like a seed carried by the wind waiting to implant itself in new soils. Cael knew of it, having dedicated his studies to the subject, but he hadn't expected to hear of it from men like the Bos, in a place like this, with so many people drawn to the simple promise of coin.
 
Cael looked around at the overtaken tavern. _Did all these people really believe it was true? That it really was up there in those hills, and they'd be the ones to slay it? Or perhaps they thought they'd be paid simply for finding that nothing was there, proving that the beast didn't exist._ Cael looked back at the Bos and thought to himself, maybe there really is others like himself here. People who came not for wealth, but for knowledge.
 
Just as Cael opened his mouth to speak again, a tankard suddenly came hurdling across the room, smacking the wall just above where they sat, splattering mead and foam all over them, although mostly on Big Bo. They all stood up, pushing out from the table.
 
"Ugh you see?!" Big Bo shouted while pointing an undersized finger at Small Bo. "We keep chasin these shite stories and always end up in shite little towns like this, way out in the ass crack of the west, packed full of fools."
 
"Oh quit yer whining ye look like me baby cousin cryin' up and down wailing about."
 
As the two Bos argued, Cael looked toward the far end of the room to see who threw that mug, when he noticed a fight had broken out near the bar, and was quickly grabbing the attention of nearly everyone in the room. Even the Bos quieted down to see the commotion.
 
The crowd suddenly split apart, many tumbled to the ground as people jumped backward trying to make space for the brawl unfolding before them. A man struggled to restrain a woman as she tried to get away from him. He held her by the arms from behind. She threw her head back into the guys face, nearly breaking his nose. The crowd cheered at that. Then the man threw her to the ground and pinned her down with his legs and began grabbing at her satchel as if trying to steal what was inside. She was pinned under his weight, but still had her legs free. She brought up her knee and rammed it into the man's back, digging in hard. He shot up arching his back groaning in pain.
 
A voice erupted from the crowd. "Get off the woman ya big brute!"
 
The man looked around, scanning the circle of spectators to see who shouted, looking from person to person trying to catch their eyes. He shouted instead.
 
"She stole my coin! She's a pick pocket!"
 
"I didn't take anything from a ye sorry ass poor bastard. No coin to take from you anyway!" She shouted from under him. He looked back down at her but she spit in his face. The man recoiled with disgust, wiping his face. "You stealin bitch!" he shouted, shoving her back down.
 
"That's where this ends," a stiff voice said, cutting in. It was the Still Man Cael saw sitting at the bar earlier. He stood tall, long dark hair trailed out behind him, bold, and direct. He held out his hand to the man on the floor, who instead of accepting the assistance, slapped the hand away and stood up to rush out of the tavern. All the heads in the room turned to watch him go. The still man then helped the woman up too, then returned to his place at the bar, back facing the crowd, and calmly took another swig of his mead.
 
The crowd slowly returned to their respective conversations, and the volume in the room slowly picked up, once again far too loud for comfort. And through the mass of people, Cael noticed the woman who was involved in the altercation. She stood there still by the bar, the man who claimed she stole from him now gone from the scene, brushing the dust off her clothing. She tugged on her shirt to adjust it back in place before reaching up to her hair, which was long and black, and removing the banding to let it fall lose. Then, interestingly, she walked along the bar over to where the Still Man, as Cael took to calling him, was seated and pulled out the stool next to him to take a seat.
 
Suddenly, Cael was slapped on the shoulder and shaken away from the sight.  
“Ho ooh easy there, lad! Young stallion eyein his mare aye? HA HA!” Small Bo was clearly entertained with this, but Cael cut in.
 
“That man.” Cael said, gesturing over to the Still Man. “Who is he?”
 
Big Bo jumped in with a chuckle, “You mean to tell me it was he you were eyein up just now?”
 
They jumped up slamming their bellies together in the air, which of course sent Big Bo bouncing away and tumbling to the floor. And so Cael laughed with them, feeling a sense of ease amongst the night’s madness, even if it was only short lived.
 
As their laughing died down, Small Bo began “Lad, we don’t know who any of these goons in this pack here, much less that mysterious fellow at the bar. Well save for you now aye? Say, how long you been standing there when we came to sit down? Have a chance to grab one of these rooms for the night?”
 
“No, I just about walked in when you saw me here.”
 
“Aw theres nothin to do about it then. Neither have we. Don’t got much coin for it either HA!” Small Bo laughed before continuing, “We found a spot quite ripe for settin up camp, just down the road on the way in here. If it please ya, yer welcome to join.”
 
Cael thought about Bo’s offer. He did, of course, still have his gear packed and tucked away in the saddlebags on his horse, even if he was looking forward to spending just one night in a real bed. He stole one more glance over at the Still Man, and the fighting woman who sat next to him. They were clearly in conversation, although not near close enough to hear. The bar woman went over to them, planting her hands on the bar counter and glancing to and from each of their faces, and now all three of them engaged in conversation. As Cael watched them from his place at the back of the tavern, that sinking feeling welled up inside him. That feeling he was too familiar with, reaching out from inside him and gripping his throat like a strangling hand. It came when he spent too long watching the lives of others, from the side, or the back, but never in the front, or the center. He supposed it’d always be that way. He’d never be the one to take the action, to have something to say that people cared to hear, and to mean it with when he said it, to grab them by the heart.
 
That’s why he started on this journey nearly two years ago, hunting down the accounts of a myth. It was to discover something that no one else had, and then he would have something to say, and maybe people would listen. There’d been nothing of substance so far, and he felt the pressure of his wasted time. But it hadn’t been so bad, even if he’d still been on the side, only hearing retellings of other people’s lives. It had taken him across the continent, to meet all sorts of people, none however, as interesting as the Bos, but still interesting nonetheless. By now, he was used to all the traveling. And even if this time it proved to be nothing more than stories, like all the other times, he knew he could still go on, chasing it. The myth:
 
A massive black beast, wings without feathers, talons and teeth sharp as steel, and the devastating capability to draw the gleam from any living thing. That was something they would listen to, and what he hoped to find.
 
And so Cael turned away from the people at the bar and looked back to the Bos, who certainly noticed him staring toward the bar again but had apparently decided not to bother him for it, as they had drifted back into their previously halted conversation on pure buffoonery.
 
“Alright.” Cael said confidently. “I’ll hold you to that offer.”
 
It was good he did because the daylight was already dwindling away as he first entered the tavern, and by the time Cael and the Bos made their way over to the previously picked spot for setting up camp, it had long past grown dark. Not as dark as Cael ever camped in, however, as on this night, the sky was clear of clouds and ablaze with the twinkling glints of the cosmos. Among it all, the moon burned bright, casting the foothills and the town below them in the familiar blue-green hues of the sea, as if everything had suddenly submerged beneath the water.
 
Cael lay on his back with his hands behind his head, recounting the day's events. The final leg of his journey to Greywharf, the over cramped tavern, and his conversation with the Bos, who now lay fast asleep nearly an arm’s length away. He was in his tent, but the open flap at the front still gave him a view of the foothills and the night sky. He stared off into the hills, at the black spot of supposed draethblight, which seemed only a patch of scorched trees in the dim moonlight. He contemplated the days to come, the trek he and almost everyone in that tavern would begin on the morrow. What would they actually find up there? As he lay there pondering the possibilities, he saw it.
 
Cael bolted upright and crawled to the edge of the tent. He crouched there in the open flap, gripping the wooden support post with one hand while shading the moon’s glare from his eyes with the other, peering out into the sky above the foothills. There he saw something strange. An amorphous blackness which blotted out the starlight, like a random drop of ink on parchment. Its shape constantly changed and shifted, and Cael struggled to make out what it was. He squinted his eyes, still trying harder. The shape drifted across the stars blocking out the light from each star in front of it, while the stars behind it blinked back into visibility. Then, the shape quickly reversed direction, traveling back the way it came. Cael quickly turned his head to look at the two tents of the Bos. He couldn’t see them, but there was no movement from within their tents, giving no indication that they had heard his sudden movement. So he looked back to the sky, but there was nothing. Or he couldn’t find it. His eyes darted back and forth across the horizon scanning for the strange sight, but he did not find it.
 
He crouched there for a few moments more before returning to lay down in his tent. He was unable to make sense of what he had just seen. Was it a stray whisp of clouds, drifting across the night sky? No, it couldn’t be. It was too fast for that, too inconsistent. He rolled over onto his side, now staring at the canvas wall of his tent, although he couldn’t actually see what was in front of him. It was just that he was in no way tired enough to close his eyes, so he just lay thinking.
 
Out of all the people who travelled to Greywharf for this bounty, whatever their reasons, it had to be him who knew the most about draeths, which wasn’t much and was assuming they really did exist. In all the stories he had collected, none of them had ever featured a draeth remaining in the same place it was first sighted for more than a few days at most. But it had been weeks since the sighting here. Cael had come knowing that if he did find something here, it would only be some simple sign, evidence of its existence. But could it really be still there, lurking in those hills? He pushed the thought aside, knowing there would be only one way to find out.
 
He would have to get some rest. They set out at first light.
