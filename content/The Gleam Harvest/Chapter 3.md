>**Someone gets a note warning them not to trust another? Its cryptic.**
 
Cael walked somewhere near the middle of the procession, clutching the leather satchel at his hip. It was midmorning, and the winding column of travelers had stretched thin along the road. Like the night before, he felt there were far too many men in one place for his liking, although the valley road was much preferable to the cramped confines of the tavern. He felt the competition brewing all around him. Too many ambitions aimed at the same target.
 
It's easy enough to reach higher grounds in the foothills by tracing the stream through the black valley, which is what the lot had taken to calling the valley above Greywharf, the place where the dark spot of death sits like a stain upon the earth. Such a trek can even be described as leisurely, but only if a man were to travel without the burden of mule-wheeled carts heavy with the equipment, like a hunting party made up of only a few men, or perhaps, a pair of small children.
 
Thus, the road that winds up and around the foothills, which takes the hills at a much lesser incline and does not present the obstacle of the forest and its underbrush, was the chosen path, though it is the longer route. Naturally, the chatter that filled the early hours had dwindled into grunts and muttered curses as wheels jostled over uneven stone and mules dug stubborn heels into the dirt. Some walked alone, heads down. Others clustered in small packs, speaking in low, eager voices about weapons, strategy, and the bounty itself. Cael had lost the Bos a while back, who seemed to make friends wherever they went, and were now somewhere near the rear of the caravan.
 
Cael was suited for this type of travel. Long days covering roads by foot or horseback. Yet the walking was getting to him. He wasn't physically tired, but mentally, cycling the details in his head over and over. _Will we really see a draeth?_ His eyes drifted to the valley below, where the stain of grey and lifeless timber blotted the forest like a drop of ink. Even from this distance, something about the place made his skin tighten.
 
“Don’t look at it,” a voice grumbled just beside him.
 
Cael startled, turning his head to see the Still Man, from the night before. Broad-shouldered, cloak drawn tight, his greatsword still strapped across his back. He stared straight ahead as they walked, unreadable expression.
